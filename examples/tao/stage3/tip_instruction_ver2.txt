AUTHORING RULES
===============
- **Stay consistent with the SRD and source document.** Use the SRD as the primary modeling requirement and do not introduce unsupported domain facts.
- **Prefer minimal and reusable modeling.** Use direct classes and relations when sufficient; introduce ODPs, reification, events, roles, or intermediate structures only when they provide a clear modeling benefit.
- **Preserve semantic distinctions.** Use subclass relations only for genuine taxonomy, keep named entities as individuals, and avoid representing the same concept redundantly in multiple forms.
- **Preserve qualification and uncertainty.** Do not convert terms such as `typically`, `generally`, `most`, `may`, or `can` into universal restrictions, exact cardinalities, or invented numeric constraints.
- **Use strong OWL axioms conservatively.** Add cardinality, equivalence, disjointness, or necessary-and-sufficient conditions only when clearly supported by the SRD or source.
- **Be precise, concise, and actionable.** Propose concrete class/property names where useful, avoid unnecessary structures or repetition, and do not output Turtle code.

- Reduce unnecessary flatness by introducing a small number of semantically meaningful and reusable parent classes for closely related concepts, even when those abstract parent classes are not explicitly named in the document.
- Prefer organizing related top-level classes under appropriate intermediate abstractions when a genuine is-a relationship can be justified.
- Do not create hierarchy merely for visual organization; every rdfs:subClassOf relation must remain semantically valid.
- Reuse these parent classes in property domains and ranges where appropriate so that the hierarchy contributes to the ontology structure rather than serving only as grouping.
- Represent unqualified or structurally expressible facts using OWL classes/properties whenever possible. Use annotations mainly for provenance, qualifiers, uncertainty, statistical statements, or conditions that cannot be faithfully represented as ordinary OWL assertions.
